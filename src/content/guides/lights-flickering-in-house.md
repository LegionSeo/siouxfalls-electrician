## Step by step: what to check when lights flicker

None of these needs tools or a cover plate removed.

1. **Count how many lights flicker.** Is it one fixture, one room, or lights all over the house? Write it down. One light, one circuit, and the whole house point to different causes.
2. **Check the bulb.** Turn the light off and let the bulb cool. Make sure it is screwed in snug, not tight. Then swap in a new bulb you know works. If the flicker stops, you're done.
3. **Look at the dimmer.** If the light is on a dimmer, check that the bulb box says "dimmable." Many older dimmers were made for old-style bulbs and flicker with LEDs, mostly at low settings. If it only flickers when dimmed, the dimmer and bulb likely don't match.
4. **Notice what else is running.** Do the lights dip when the furnace blower, a space heater, the microwave, or the washer starts? A short dip when a big motor starts can be normal. A deep dim, or one that keeps happening, can mean the circuit is overloaded or a connection is loose.
5. **Check with the neighbors.** If lights flicker all over the house, ask a neighbor if theirs are doing it too, or check your utility's outage map. If they are, call your power company first. The problem may be on their side.
6. **Look, listen, and sniff, but don't open anything.** A buzzing switch, a warm cover plate, scorch marks, or a burning smell means stop. Turn that light off and leave it off. Don't take the cover off the panel.
7. **Call with what you found.** If a new bulb didn't fix it, or flicker shows up in more than one room, the problem is in a switch, a fixture, the wiring, or the panel. Your notes from steps 1 to 6 save time.

If you see smoke or flames, leave the house and call 911 first.

Flicker that comes and goes deserves more attention than a light that is simply dead. On-and-off power often means a loose connection, and a loose connection can make heat inside a box while the light still seems to work.

## Cold-weather causes in Sioux Falls homes

Most national guides on flickering lights skip winter. In a Sioux Falls winter, these come up again and again:

- **Space heaters.** A plug-in heater pulls a lot of power. If it shares a circuit with lights, the lights can dim each time it cycles on. Plug it straight into the wall, give it its own circuit if you can, and never run it on an extension cord.
- **The furnace blower.** A quick dip when the blower starts can be normal. If the dip is deep, happens all over the house, or keeps getting worse, have the circuit and panel checked.
- **Wind and ice on the service line.** Many homes get power from an overhead line to the house. Strong wind or ice can strain that line or its connections. If flicker shows up during a storm and the whole house is affected, call your power company and keep clear of any line that is down or sagging.
- **Holiday lighting.** Strings of lights and inflatables plugged into the same outdoor or garage outlet can overload a circuit. Spread them out over more than one circuit.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why are my lights flickering in just one room?

A loose or failing bulb, a dimmer that doesn't match LED bulbs, or a worn switch is the usual cause. Swap the bulb first. If more than one light in the room flickers, a loose connection on that circuit is likely, and an electrician should test it.

### Is it normal for lights to dim when the furnace or a space heater turns on?

A short, small dip when a big motor or heater starts can be normal. A deep dim, flicker all over the house, or dimming that is getting worse is not. Move space heaters to their own circuit and have the circuit and panel checked.

### Why are the lights flickering in my whole house?

Flicker in every room often points to the panel, the meter, or the line from the utility. Ask a neighbor if they see it too. If they do, call your power company. If only your house has it, call an electrician, because a loose main connection can overheat.

### Are flickering lights dangerous?

One flickering bulb usually isn't. Flicker that comes with buzzing, a warm switch or cover plate, scorch marks, a burning smell, or lights that get brighter while others dim can mean a loose or arcing connection. Turn that light off and call an electrician.

### Do LED bulbs flicker on old dimmer switches?

Often, yes. Many older dimmers were made for old-style bulbs and don't control LEDs smoothly, which causes flicker or buzzing at low settings. Use bulbs marked dimmable and a dimmer rated for LEDs. An electrician can swap the dimmer.
