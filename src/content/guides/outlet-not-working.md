## Step by step: what to check when an outlet stops working

None of these needs tools or a cover plate removed.

1. **Make sure it is the outlet, not the device.** Plug a lamp or phone charger you know works into the dead outlet. Then try your original device in an outlet that works.
2. **Test both halves and the outlets around it.** Try the top and bottom of the dead outlet, then every outlet in the room and the rooms next to it. Write down what is dead. One outlet, half an outlet, and a whole row point to different causes.
3. **Flip the wall switches.** Many living rooms and bedrooms have an outlet where one half is controlled by a wall switch, meant for a lamp. If a switch by the door doesn't seem to do anything, flip it and test again.
4. **Find and reset every GFCI outlet.** A GFCI is the outlet with TEST and RESET buttons. Look in the kitchen, bathrooms, garage, basement, laundry area, and on outside walls. One GFCI can cut power to ordinary outlets farther down the same circuit, even in a different room. Press RESET firmly until it clicks and stays in.
5. **Check the breaker panel.** A tripped breaker often sits in the middle, between ON and OFF. Push it all the way to OFF, then back to ON. Do this once. Some breakers also have a TEST button (these are AFCI or GFCI breakers) and reset the same way. Don't take the panel cover off.
6. **Look, sniff, and feel the cover plate, but don't open it.** Brown or black marks, melted plastic, a cracked face, a buzzing sound, a warm plate, or a burning smell all mean stop. Leave that outlet unplugged and turn its breaker off if you can reach it safely.
7. **Call with what you found.** If nothing above brings the outlet back, the problem is behind the wall: a loose or failed connection, a worn-out outlet, a failed breaker, or damaged wire. Your notes from steps 2 to 5 save time.

If you see smoke or fire, leave the house and call 911 first.

## What the symptoms usually mean

| What you notice | Common cause | Safe for you to check | Time to call an electrician |
|---|---|---|---|
| Only the top or bottom half is dead | Half of the outlet is on a wall switch | Flip the switches in the room | No switch brings it back |
| Several outlets in a row are dead, breaker looks on | Tripped GFCI upstream, or a loose connection at the first dead outlet | Reset every GFCI in the house | No GFCI reset restores power |
| GFCI won't reset at all | No power reaching it, a fault on the circuit, or a failed GFCI | Check the breaker for that area | Breaker is on and it still won't reset |
| GFCI resets, then trips again (often garage or outdoors) | Moisture in an outdoor box, or a fault in something plugged in | Unplug everything on it, let a wet box dry, reset once | It trips with nothing plugged in |
| Breaker trips again right after one reset | A short, a ground fault, or too much load | Unplug heaters and other big loads, reset once | It trips again: leave it off |
| Plugs fall out or only work when wiggled | Worn contacts inside the outlet | Stop using it | Soon. Loose contacts can heat up |
| Warm plate, scorch marks, buzzing, burning smell | Loose connection or arcing | Nothing. Stop using it | Now |

An outlet that works only some of the time deserves more attention than one that is fully dead. On-and-off power often comes from a loose connection, and a loose connection can make heat inside the box while the outlet still seems to work.

## Winter causes in Sioux Falls homes

Most national guides on dead outlets skip the cold-weather causes. In a Sioux Falls winter, these come up again and again:

- **Space heaters.** Most plug-in space heaters draw up to 1,500 watts, a large share of what one ordinary household circuit is built to carry. Add a hair dryer or microwave on the same circuit and the breaker trips, taking every outlet on it down. Give a space heater its own outlet, plug it straight into the wall, and don't run it on an extension cord.
- **Outdoor outlets and snowmelt.** Holiday lights, heated pet bowls, and outdoor extension cords usually plug into a GFCI-protected outlet. Melting snow, frost, or a cover left open can let moisture in and trip it. Unplug everything, let the box dry, and reset once. If it keeps tripping when dry, have it checked.
- **Garage circuits.** Garage outlets are often GFCI-protected, and in winter they carry engine block heaters, battery chargers, and heated tools. A tripped garage GFCI can also take down other outlets on the same run. If a freezer or other important appliance shares that circuit, check it after any trip, and ask an electrician about the setup. Never bypass a GFCI to keep it from tripping.
- **After a power outage.** When the power comes back after a winter storm, check your GFCIs and breakers before you assume an outlet failed.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is my outlet not working when the breaker isn't tripped?

Most often a GFCI outlet somewhere else on the circuit has tripped. Reset every GFCI in the house, including the garage and outdoors. Also try the wall switches. If power still doesn't return, a loose connection or a worn outlet is likely, and an electrician should test it.

### Can a GFCI in another room shut off my outlet?

Yes. One GFCI can protect ordinary outlets farther down the same circuit, sometimes in another room. When it trips, all of those outlets lose power. Pressing RESET on that one GFCI brings them all back.

### Why does my outdoor outlet stop working in winter?

Outdoor outlets are usually GFCI-protected, and moisture from melting snow or frost can trip them. Holiday lights and outdoor cords can trip them too. Unplug everything, let the box dry, and reset once. If it trips with nothing plugged in, have it checked.

### Why did my outlets stop working after a power outage?

The outage, or the power coming back, may have tripped a GFCI or a breaker. Reset those first. If an outlet is still dead after that, a connection may have failed. Call an electrician rather than opening the outlet.

### Is a dead outlet dangerous?

A dead outlet alone is not always dangerous. Heat, scorch marks, buzzing, a burning smell, or power that comes and goes are warning signs of a loose or arcing connection. Stop using it, turn off its breaker if you can reach it safely, and call.
